# Model evaluation

## Concept

Swapping the model behind a system, or changing its prompt or its retrieval, can make answers worse while every operational signal stays green. An eval gate decides before promotion instead of after: it runs the current baseline and the candidate on the same golden set, a fixed list of questions whose correct answer is known in advance, scores each answer, and averages the scores. The candidate is promoted when its mean is no lower than the baseline's by more than a tolerance, and blocked otherwise. A canary release answers a different question, how to switch versions without downtime, and only warns once real traffic reaches the new version.

The score must be computable without a human. Groundedness compares the generated answer with the expected text, here as the cosine similarity of their embeddings ([text-embeddings](text-embeddings.md)), so an answer that stays faithful to the expected clause scores close to 1. Two similar models land close to each other, and sampling noise alone can flip that decision between runs, which is why the gate decides and not a glance at the answers. A broken configuration, such as retrieval that silently stops injecting context, drops the score far past the tolerance every time. The metric has a known blind spot: it cannot tell a faithful citation from an answer that only echoes the clause, which in a regulatory domain is often the correct behavior.

A model tuned to extract fields as JSON is scored on two levels. Schema adequacy asks whether the answer parses as a single object with exactly the expected keys, none missing and none extra, and per-field precision compares each value with the answer key, numbers within a small tolerance and text after removing accents, case, and extra spaces, so a correct answer is not counted wrong for its spelling. The test set must be held out, examples the model never saw in training, and handwritten invariance rounds change the input's form while keeping its content, such as other labels, running text, an amount in words, or two records in one text, to show whether the model learned the task or the template. Asking the same input several times at temperature 0 and counting the distinct answers shows whether a result is stable. A model retrained on more data is measured again with the same harness and sets, because more data can introduce a regression the pilot did not have.

A tuned model earns its cost only when it beats the generic model it came from, on the same test with the same prompt. Adding a request for bare JSON to the generic model's prompt separates the two things tuning bought, the format and the content. With a handful of examples a difference of means can be noise, so a paired bootstrap resamples the per-example differences with replacement thousands of times and reads the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles of the resampled means: an interval wholly above zero is an advantage sampling does not explain, and one that crosses zero is not. Training two domains in one model is weighed the same way, against one model per domain on each domain's share of the test.

When the task has no single right answer, such as a written claim review, a second model judges which of two answers is better under a rubric and answers in JSON. Judges have known biases, so the same pair is judged again in the swapped order, and a verdict that follows the position instead of the content is not trusted. A judge from another model checks self-preference, and a correct answer in a broken format is rewritten as prose by a purely syntactic extraction and judged again, to tell a loss of format from a loss of reasoning. A rubric also separates two answers that a binary check passes alike, such as two refusals that leak nothing.

A probe with questions outside the domain shows whether the tuned model still answers anything else, and whether its habit of answering in JSON leaks there. One pass over six hard examples is weak evidence, since a small sample can suggest an error a larger one does not confirm, so a statistical remeasurement repeats a round N times and counts errors over every call. Scaling is then decided by a checklist fixed before measuring: each criterion has a threshold, each measuring tool records its result in a shared ledger, and the verdict reads the ledger instead of numbers copied by hand, warning when a criterion falls back to historical evidence or when its tool changed after the measurement. Only a case that passes the decision gate ([decision-framework](decision-framework.md)) and is covered by the measured evidence scales, and a task the evaluation never tested does not inherit it.

## How the course implements it

The enterprise architecture lesson of the AI systems architecture module writes the eval gate twice, in **JavaScript**, the official version, and in **Python**, its mirror, with the **ollama** client 0.6 in both. It takes no arguments and reads nothing.

The golden set holds three questions about a clinical trial, each with its expected clause and the clause's source: the assent rules for minors, the right to withdraw at any time, and the minimum age. The baseline is gemma4:e2b, the candidate gemma4:e2b-mlx, the embedding model nomic-embed-text, and the tolerance 0.02. Evaluating a model asks it each question in turn through **Ollama**'s native chat without streaming ([llm-http-api](llm-http-api.md)), with a system prompt that asks for short, precise answers citing the given regulatory source and a user prompt that holds the question, the clause, and its source. The answer and the clause are then embedded, **JavaScript** sending both requests at once and **Python** one after the other, and their cosine is the item's score, printed as [Eval], the question's first 55 characters followed by ..., and the score with three decimals. The model's score is the mean of the three.

The program evaluates the baseline, then the candidate as scenario 1, and then, as scenario 2, the baseline again without the clause: the system prompt drops the request to cite the source and the user prompt holds only the question. Each scenario prints the candidate's mean, the difference from the baseline with a plus sign when it is not negative, the tolerance as -0.02, and the decision, PROMOVE when the difference is at least -0.02 and BLOQUEIA otherwise, followed by a fixed remark. Scenario 1 is the borderline comparison of two real models, and scenario 2, the regression a configuration bug causes, is blocked.

The model evaluation lesson of the data processing and fine-tuning module writes six tools twice, in **JavaScript**, the official version, and in **Python**, its mirror, with only the standard library apart from **MLX-LM**. They import the module's earlier lessons by relative paths, as the course lays them out: the fine-tuning via API lesson's example generator and its reassessment of Saúde Empresarial, the decision framework lesson's gate and NPV, and the LoRA lesson's rank 8 adapter. The tools that call **Vertex AI** run gcloud auth print-access-token before every call and send generateContent ([llm-http-api](llm-http-api.md)) to us-central1, with the instruction, a blank line, and the input as the user turn and temperature 0.

The harness builds its held-out set with the generator at index 5000 and up, one example per source, six workshop estimates and five medical receipts, none seen in training. Schema adequacy strips a Markdown fence around the whole answer, rejects anything that is not an object, and lists the missing and the extra keys, and per-field precision compares numbers within 0.01, after converting a string, and text after NFD normalization without combining marks, lowercased, trimmed, and with whitespace collapsed. Its eleven self-checks end with a live call on one more example, at index 5999, that must answer a valid schema with at least two of three fields right. It then requires ENDPOINT_MODULO32, the tuned model's endpoint, sends the eleven examples, printing each one's schema and precision, sets apart a failed call, scores an invalid schema as precision 0, and prints the valid schemas over the answered calls and the mean precision. It records the lower of the two rates, as a rounded percentage, as the baseline criterion of resultado-medido.json beside it, and finally asks the first example three times and counts the distinct answers.

The A/B tool, which also requires GCP_PROJECT_ID, compares the tuned endpoint with gemini-2.5-flash, first with the same prompt and then with a request for bare JSON appended to the generic model's instruction, and bootstraps the eleven paired differences of precision with that hint, 10,000 resamples at 95% from an unseeded generator. Its judge section asks both models for a two-to-three-sentence review of two handwritten claims, an anomalous amount and a beneficiary distractor with a missing prior authorization, and asks gemini-2.5-flash which review is better, sending the case, Resposta 1, and Resposta 2 under a rubric that wants {"melhor": "resposta_1" or "resposta_2", "justificativa": ...}, up to three times when the verdict is not JSON. It judges the pair again in both orders, asks gemini-2.5-pro, and judges once more with the tuned review rewritten as prose, its compativel and pendencias read by regular expressions from the nested, malformed JSON the tuned model writes, from a valid object, or from the prose of its parecer field, negation first. Two fixed cases need only the judge, in both orders: an archived Arena-Hard-Auto prompt with its GPT-4-0314 and GPT-3.5-turbo answers, and two refusals of a request for the system prompt. When ENDPOINT_AUTO_ONLY and ENDPOINT_SAUDE_ONLY name the models tuned on one domain each, from the 120 and 80 examples of the lesson's two JSONL files in **Vertex AI**'s tuning format ([model-fine-tuning](model-fine-tuning.md)), it compares the joint model with each on its own domain's examples. Its argument medir-graduacao with N, 20 by default, repeats the hinted A/B and that comparison N times and records the bate-generico and junto-bate-separado criteria, the tuned model's worst result against its rival's best. A 429 is retried after 2, 4, and 8 seconds, four attempts in all.

The stress test takes a mode as its first argument: round0, four questions outside the domain asked of the tuned model and then of the generic one, round1, the baseline on the held-out set followed by six texts with other labels, a decoy name, or running text, which records the robusto-formato criterion with its drop against the baseline, round2, six texts with a chatty message, an amount in words, or two records in one, which asks the first wrong example three more times to see whether the error is stable, and round2-medir with N, 58 by default, which repeats round 2 N times and records robusto-estrutura. Without an argument it runs its self-checks and rounds 0, 1, and 2. The local evaluation scores the same held-out set on mlx-community/gemma-4-e2b-it-bf16 with the LoRA lesson's rank 8 adapter ([parameter-efficient-fine-tuning](parameter-efficient-fine-tuning.md)): **Python** loads it once with mlx_lm and generates up to 150 tokens through the tokenizer's chat template, and **JavaScript** runs python3 -m mlx_lm generate once per example, reloading the model each time, takes the text between the first two lines of equals signs, and prints how long each call took. A **Google Colab** notebook trains the same adapter with **PEFT** and **TRL** in 4 bits on a T4 GPU and scores it with copies of the harness's metrics.

The NPV tool reopens the decision framework lesson's NPV ([investment-analysis](investment-analysis.md)) with the measured training costs, R$1.53 for Auto and R$0.86 for Saúde Empresarial, in place of the R$2,400 estimate. The scale verdict reopens the four-question gate ([decision-framework](decision-framework.md)) for Auto, for Saúde Empresarial nine months later, and for Atendimento ao Cliente, reads the five criteria from the ledger, and scales Auto and Saúde Empresarial while Atendimento ao Cliente stays blocked by its red questions. Its four self-checks expect every criterion to pass, so a criterion below its threshold ends it with an error instead of a verdict.

The final project lesson of the data processing and fine-tuning module verifies its scaled model, gemini-2.5-flash tuned on 3,000 examples ([dataset-preparation](dataset-preparation.md)), against the pilot tuned on 200 ([model-fine-tuning](model-fine-tuning.md)), in **JavaScript** and in **Python** with only the standard library. It reuses the model evaluation lesson's harness by a relative path, its held-out set, schema adequacy, and per-field precision, and keeps in the tool two invariance rounds of six handwritten texts each, three per task, the same as the stress test's: other labels and running text, then a chatty message, an amount in words, and two records in one text, one of them incomplete. It requires ENDPOINT_MODULO32 and ENDPOINT_MODULO63, the pilot's and the scaled model's endpoints, as soon as it loads, before its three self-checks. For each model, pilot first, it sends every example of the three sets in order, one generateContent call each on the endpoint ([llm-http-api](llm-http-api.md)), with the instruction, a blank line, and the input as the user turn and temperature 0, scores the answer, giving precision 0 to an invalid schema, and prints each set's valid schemas out of its size and mean precision as a percentage with one decimal. The lesson's documented result has both models at 100% on the held-out set and the first round, while the scaled model answers both two-record texts of the second round as JSON arrays, 4/6 and 66.7%. A failed call ends the run with exit code 1.

## What usually goes wrong in other languages

- The clause is embedded again for every item of every evaluation, nine times in all, beside the nine answers, and nothing is cached, so the cases expect eighteen embedding requests.
- **JavaScript**'s concurrent pair of embeddings reaches the server in either order, while **Python** sends the answer first. The replay answers both by their text, so a transcription may do either.
- **JavaScript** embeds through the legacy /api/embeddings endpoint and **Python** through /api/embed, whose vectors are normalized. The cosine ignores the length, so both print the same scores.
- The question is cut to its first 55 characters, which **JavaScript** counts in UTF-16 units and **Python** in code points, the same for these accented questions. Slicing a UTF-8 string by bytes, as **Go**, **Rust**, and **C++** do by default, cuts them elsewhere and can split a character, so those count runes or characters.
- The difference keeps its sign through rounding: a tiny negative difference prints as -0.000 in both originals, and a non-negative one gets a plus sign.
- A difference of exactly -0.02 promotes, since the comparison is greater than or equal.
- Neither version raises when a scenario is blocked: the decision is printed, the program ends normally, and the process exits with 0.
- A JSON array with the right objects inside is not an adequate schema. **Python** checks isinstance(obj, dict) and **JavaScript** rejects arrays and null explicitly, so a transcription never unwraps a one-element array or takes its first object, which would turn the scaled model's 4/6 into 6/6.
- Removing accents means decomposing to NFD and dropping the combining marks, which **JavaScript** does with String.normalize and **Python** with unicodedata. **Go** needs golang.org/x/text/unicode/norm, **Rust** **unicode-normalization**, and **C++** **ICU** or **utf8proc**, while **C#** and **Java** normalize in their standard libraries.
- An amount that does not parse as a number is a miss, never an error: **JavaScript**'s Number gives NaN, which compares false, and **Python** catches the ValueError of float.
- The final project reads its endpoints when the program loads, so a missing one stops it before the self-checks with an uncaught error and its stack trace, while the evaluation lesson's tools check theirs only before their first call, after the self-checks, whose live check then fails. An empty variable counts as missing in both.
- **JavaScript**'s fetch resolves an error status, which the harness reports as Falha ao chamar o modelo: with the status and its reason, while **Python**'s urllib raises and the harness prints HTTP Error with the same two. The final project ends there with Erro: on standard error in **JavaScript** and on standard output in **Python**, and the evaluation lesson's harness prints the example's [FALHOU] line and goes on. The final project reads the address from GOOGLE_VERTEX_BASE_URL, falling back to the regional endpoint, while the evaluation lesson's tools hardcode the us-central1 address, which their cases replace with the same variable, and gcloud auth print-access-token runs before every call.
- **Python**'s A/B tool writes its own texts without accents, the review and judge instructions, the cases, the refusals, the hint, and most of what it prints, while the examples from the generator keep theirs, so its requests differ from **JavaScript**'s only in those texts, and the cases accept either spelling.
- The bootstrap draws from an unseeded generator, so its interval moves between runs while the observed mean does not. The bounds are the sorted means at floor(0.025 × 10,000) = 250 and floor(0.975 × 10,000), which floating point makes 9,749.
- The position swap maps the second verdict back to the models: resposta_1 in the swapped order is the generic review, so a consistent judge names the same model in both orders.
- Only HTTP 429 is retried. Any other status, or the fourth 429, fails the call, and the A/B tool reports it as Falha ao chamar with the resource, the status, and its reason in both versions. A judge's answer that is not JSON after stripping a fence is asked again with the same prompt, with a notice, and the third failure ends that review case with [FALHOU] while the program goes on.
- The ledger is one JSON file shared by four tools, rewritten under an exclusive .lock file through a temporary file renamed over it, so a tool replaces only its own key. **Python** writes whole percentages as 100.0 and **JavaScript** as 100, the date is the local date as YYYY-MM-DD, and the verdict reads either.
- The verdict warns when a measuring tool's file was modified after the end of its measurement day, which a fresh checkout always is, and names that tool's own file, so a transcription names its own. A missing ledger or key falls back to the evidence fixed in the code, which differs from the shipped ledger, 72.7% instead of 66.7% for the generic model with the hint.
- **JavaScript** passes the whole prompt, newlines included, as one argument of mlx_lm's CLI, and the adapter as an absolute path resolved from the program's folder. The CLI prints the generation between two lines of ten equals signs, then its speed and memory, and any other layout fails the call.
- The stress test's modes print different self-checks: round0 and the default add the probe's two to the four, round1 and round2 print only the four, and round2-medir prints none. round2 hides the baseline it still measures.
- The verdict's self-checks throw after printing their totals, so a failed criterion exits with code 1 and the count of failed checks on standard error in both versions, **JavaScript** prefixed with Erro: and **Python** through SystemExit.

## How close common targets get

The gate and the harness are loops, means, and comparisons around the model calls, so every target is a direct equivalent, with the clients of [llm-http-api](llm-http-api.md) and, for the harness, a Unicode normalizer:

| Target                            | Fit               | Library                                                                                                         |
| --------------------------------- | ----------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Python**                        | Direct equivalent | **ollama**, as the original, or urllib.request, subprocess, and unicodedata for the harness                     |
| **JavaScript** and **TypeScript** | Direct equivalent | **ollama**, built with the host from OLLAMA_HOST, or fetch, child_process, and String.normalize for the harness |
| **Go**                            | Direct equivalent | net/http, encoding/json, os/exec, and golang.org/x/text/unicode/norm                                            |
| **Rust**                          | Direct equivalent | **reqwest** with blocking and JSON support, **serde_json**, and **unicode-normalization**                       |
| **C#**                            | Direct equivalent | HttpClient, System.Text.Json, and string.Normalize                                                              |
| **Java**                          | Direct equivalent | The built-in HTTP client, **Jackson**, and java.text.Normalizer                                                 |
| **C++**                           | Direct equivalent | **libcurl**, **nlohmann/json**, and **utf8proc** or **ICU**                                                     |
| **Swift**                         | Direct equivalent | **Foundation**'s URLSession, Process, JSONSerialization, and decomposedStringWithCanonicalMapping               |

The bootstrap and the judge need nothing beyond the language's random generator and JSON parser. The local evaluation is the exception: **MLX-LM** exists only for **Python** on Apple Silicon, so every other target only has a substitute, running python3 -m mlx_lm generate once per example as the **JavaScript** version does, which pays a model load per call.

A tested **Swift** transcription of the evaluation lesson's harness, with a port of the fine-tuning via API lesson's generator and nothing beyond **Foundation**, writes its ledger with JSONSerialization's sorted keys, which the verdict reads all the same.

## Tolerance

Two similar models can swap places between runs, so the cases fix the answers in a recording, with gemma4:12b-mlx standing in for both gemma4 variants, and replay their embeddings. Every score, mean, difference, and decision is then compared exactly at the three decimals the gate prints: the baseline averages 0.922, the candidate 0.924 and is promoted at +0.002, and the baseline without the clause 0.801 and is blocked at -0.121. The two embedding endpoints' vectors move a score by less than 1e-8, and every printed value stays at least 3e-5 from a rounding boundary.

No tuned endpoint of the evaluation lesson or the final project was reachable, so their answers are synthesized from **Vertex AI**'s generateContent format to reproduce each lesson's documented result, with variations only the normalization accepts, such as a capitalized or accented answer, and failures that exercise each branch, such as the scaled model's two arrays or a 429. The evaluation lesson's generic model and judges are synthesized from the behavior, answers, and verdicts that lesson documents. The schema counts, percentages, verdicts, and ledger entries are then compared exactly, apart from the ledger's date, and the requests by their endpoint, prompt, and temperature, in order.

The bootstrap is random, but its bounds land on discrete means: with seven paired differences of 1/3, two of 0, and two of 2/3, every resampled mean is a multiple of 1/33, and the 251st and 9,750th of 10,000 sorted means fall on 21.2 and 45.5 points more than six standard deviations away from the neighboring values, so the interval is compared exactly. The local evaluation's timings are free, while its schema counts and precisions are exact, since **MLX-LM** generates greedily.
