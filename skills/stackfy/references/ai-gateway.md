# AI gateway

## Concept

An AI gateway sits between the callers and the models and handles what every request needs regardless of which model answers. It classifies the request's intent, and the intent then decides the rest: which model is worth the cost ([model-routing](model-routing.md)), which index holds the relevant knowledge ([hybrid-search](hybrid-search.md)), whether a cached answer may be reused, and whether a human must approve the result ([decision-framework](decision-framework.md)). A semantic cache keeps answered questions with their embeddings ([text-embeddings](text-embeddings.md)) and answers a new question with the cached answer when their cosine similarity reaches a threshold, which is calibrated for one embedding model and one language: between the similarity of close paraphrases and that of unrelated questions, with room on both sides. Answers whose error would be costly or irreversible never enter or leave the cache. Streaming shows the answer as it is generated instead of after it is complete, and an audit trail records every decision, including a pending approval before anyone answers it ([agent-observability](agent-observability.md)). Shared by many tenants, such as teams or studies, the gateway also enforces a budget per tenant: each request reserves its worst-case cost before any model call and gives back what it did not spend, so concurrent requests of one tenant cannot all pass a check that none of them has paid for yet. A provider's prompt cache, which reuses the processing of a repeated prompt prefix, is a different pattern that only paid APIs bill and that a local server does not show.

## How the course implements it

The specific AI patterns lesson of the AI systems architecture module closes with the TrialForge gateway prototype, written in **JavaScript**, the official version, and in **Python**, its mirror, with the **ollama** client 0.6 in both. It takes no arguments and runs five fixed questions in sequence, after twelve self-checks and the indexing of its clauses. The answers to the approval gate come from standard input.

The self-checks print an [OK] or [FALHOU] line each, under two titles, and a total: seven questions through the intent classifier, the cosine of identical, orthogonal, and opposite vectors, and the two retrieval checks of [hybrid-search](hybrid-search.md). Any failure raises before the first model call.

Each question gets the id req-1, req-2, and so on, cache hits included, and goes through the same steps, each printing a line named after its pattern:

1. The intent comes from the lowercase question by substring, in order: csr, relatório final, síntese, evento adverso, or desfecho make it sintese_csr, then critério, inclusão, exclusão, idade mínima, or protocolo make it consulta_protocolo, and anything else is consulta_icf.
2. The question is embedded once, with nomic-embed-text, and that vector serves the cache and the retrieval.
3. A sintese_csr question skips the cache. Any other is compared with every cached question, the best similarity starting at 0, and an entry at 0.75 or more is a hit: the gateway prints HIT with the similarity, audits the hit, and returns the cached answer without calling a model. Otherwise it prints MISS with the best similarity, 0.000 for an empty cache.
4. The intent picks the model, gemma4:latest for sintese_csr and gemma4:e2b for the rest ([model-routing](model-routing.md)), and the index, and the agentic hybrid search returns the clause and its confidence.
5. The native chat streams the answer from a system prompt and a user prompt that holds the question, the clause's texto, and its fonte ([llm-http-api](llm-http-api.md)). Only each chunk's content is printed and kept as the draft, so a reasoning model's thinking never shows.
6. A sintese_csr draft always goes to the Approval Gate, and any other when the confidence is below 0.7 ([decision-framework](decision-framework.md)). The pending request is audited before the question is asked.
7. The final record audits the decision. A rejected draft is dropped, and an approved draft of a routine intent joins the cache with the question's embedding.

The five questions exercise every path: a routine question about the assent of minors misses the empty cache and is confident at once, its paraphrase hits the cache, the CSR synthesis takes the expensive model and the mandatory gate, a question about storing biological samples matches no clause well enough after three iterations and is escalated, and a question about the minimum age is routed to the protocol index and confident at once. The thresholds were calibrated with nomic-embed-text on Portuguese questions, where close paraphrases scored about 0.82 and unrelated questions about 0.64.

After the five questions, the gateway reads the trail back and checks its decisions instead of trusting what it printed ([agent-observability](agent-observability.md)). An unhandled error prints [Erro não tratado] with its message, on standard error in **JavaScript** and standard output in **Python**, audits a technical failure, and exits with 1.

The enterprise architecture lesson extends the gateway into the tiering prototype, again in **JavaScript**, the official version, and in **Python**, its mirror, with the **ollama** client 0.6. It keeps two clauses, assent and withdrawal, each indexed once by its theme and its text, the streamed answer, the Approval Gate, and the audit trail, and drops the cache and the hybrid search: the clause whose theme is closest to the question by cosine is retrieved. The intent is sintese_csr when the lowercase question contains csr, relatório final, or síntese, and consulta_clausula otherwise. Seven self-checks of the intent, the cosine, and the budget check run first, and any failure raises before the first model call.

Every request names its study, and each study has a budget, 0.05 for estudo-A and 0.005 for estudo-B, against illustrative costs of 0.001 for a cheap call and 0.01 for an expensive one. Before embedding anything, the gateway reserves the request's worst case, 0.011 for a routine question, which may call both tiers, and 0.01 for a CSR synthesis, checking and debiting in one step. A reservation that would pass the limit blocks the request: it prints BLOQUEADO and audits bloqueado_por_orcamento without any model call. Otherwise a cascade picks the tier ([model-routing](model-routing.md)), the gateway gives back what the request did not spend, asks for approval only for a CSR synthesis ([decision-framework](decision-framework.md)), audits the decision, and prints the study's spend with four decimals against its limit. Four requests in sequence show the three behaviors the lesson asks for: estudo-A's routine question stays on the cheap tier, its off-topic question escalates, its CSR synthesis goes straight to the expensive tier and is approved, and estudo-B, whose budget fits no reservation, is blocked. The prototype then reads the trail back and verifies its last four records ([agent-observability](agent-observability.md)). An unhandled error prints [Erro não tratado] and audits a technical failure, as in the gateway, except that **Python** exits with 0.

The **JavaScript** version also takes --volume, which runs the self-checks and the indexing and then fires seventeen requests at once through Promise.all, with no CSR synthesis and so no approval: four for each of estudo-C, estudo-D, and estudo-E, which have 0.05 each, the assent question twice, the withdrawal question, and the off-topic question, and five assent questions for estudo-F, whose limit of 0.0225 fits exactly two reservations. Every reservation happens at dispatch, before any request finishes, so three of estudo-F's requests are blocked. The volume mode then prints each study's spend and limit with four decimals, marked [OK] or [ESTOUROU], and a closing verdict, and raises when any study passed its limit. The **Python** version is synchronous, has no volume mode, and runs its four requests whatever its arguments.

The final project lesson of the data processing and fine-tuning module puts the smallest gateway in front of its tuned extraction model: the Amplitude Seguros assistant, in **JavaScript** only, as the lesson asks, with no dependencies. It takes the user's text as its arguments, joined by spaces, and two flags, --local and --skip-tests, and any other argument starting with -- prints Flag desconhecida with the accepted flags and the usage on standard error and exits with 1 ([command-line-interface](command-line-interface.md)). Unless --skip-tests is given, fourteen self-checks run first, one of them sending a workshop estimate to the tuned endpoint, and a failure ends the program with exit code 1. Without text, it then prints the usage and ends.

The intent is the domain. The lowercase text scores a point for each of nine workshop words (placa, veículo, veiculo, oficina, funilaria, para-choque, parachoque, lataria, and pintura) and eleven health words (beneficiário, beneficiario, procedimento, clínica, clinica, consulta, exame, hospital, paciente, convênio, and convenio) it contains as a substring, once per word however often it appears. No point on either side is out of scope and a tie is ambiguous, and neither calls a model: the first hands the text to a human specialist and the second asks the user which domain it is. Otherwise the higher score picks amplitude-auto or amplitude-saude-empresarial and that task's instruction. The text goes to the tuned **Gemini** endpoint in ENDPOINT_MODULO32 through the model evaluation lesson's harness, the same generateContent call the lesson's verification tool makes ([model-evaluation](model-evaluation.md)), or, with --local, to chamar_modelo_local.py beside the program, started as python3 with the instruction and the text as JSON on standard input, which answers with the module's MLX checkpoint ([parameter-efficient-fine-tuning](parameter-efficient-fine-tuning.md)). The answer is validated twice: by the harness's schema adequacy, then by a consistency check that the amount is a finite number and both text fields are non-empty strings, since checking the keys alone lets a non-numeric amount through as R$ NaN. Each failure is reported as schema_invalido with its own message, and a success as a confirmation of the fields with the amount in Brazilian currency, as R$ 1.870,50, and a question asking the user to confirm. The outcome prints as one line: its kind in brackets, what answered in parentheses (Vertex AI, modelo local, or nenhum modelo chamado, filtro de classificação), and the message. A failed call ends the program with Erro: and the reason on standard error, a hint for the path taken, and exit code 1.

## What usually goes wrong in other languages

- The cache's best similarity starts at 0 and only a strictly higher one replaces it, so an empty cache prints 0.000 and a negative similarity never hits.
- The question is embedded once, before the cache lookup, even when it hits, so a cache hit still costs one embedding request, and a sintese_csr question is embedded although it skips the cache.
- Only approved drafts of routine intents enter the cache, after the audit, and a sintese_csr answer never does, approved or not.
- The thresholds belong to nomic-embed-text in Portuguese. Another embedding model, such as one from a paid provider, needs its own calibration, so transcriptions keep the model, and the cases replay its vectors.
- The intent classifier matches substrings of the lowercase question, accents included, so síntese matches and sintese does not, and the order of the two checks decides a question that names both a CSR and a criterion.
- **C#** and **Java** format numbers in the machine's culture, so a Brazilian locale prints 0,803. Transcriptions format with the invariant culture, as the tested **C#** one does with FormattableString.Invariant.
- The audit trail's path is the program's own folder, \_\_dirname in **JavaScript** and \_\_file\_\_'s folder in **Python**. A compiled target finds its source folder at build time, as **C#**'s CallerFilePath does, since its binary runs from a build folder.
- The budget reservation checks and debits with nothing in between that could let another request run. **JavaScript** gets that from one synchronous step on its single thread, as **Python**'s asyncio would, but targets whose concurrent requests run on threads, such as **Go**, **Rust**, **Java**, and **C#**, hold a mutex around both steps or loop on a compare-and-swap. Checking first and debiting after the first model call lets every concurrent request of a study pass, which is the bug the volume mode exists to catch.
- A request reserves its worst case, not its expected cost, so estudo-F admits only two of its five requests although each spends 0.001 in the end, and giving back the rest never unblocks a request already refused.
- The spend line prints the limit as the number's shortest form, 0.05, while the volume summary prints both figures with four decimals, as 0.0500 and 0.0225.
- In the volume mode, the concurrent streams print their chunks as they arrive, so their lines interleave, and each request appends its trail record when it finishes, in completion order.
- The assistant's keywords count once each, by substring of the lowercase text, so placas counts as placa, and the accented and unaccented spellings are separate words. Lowercasing must handle accented capitals, as **JavaScript**'s toLowerCase, **Python**'s lower, and **Go**'s strings.ToLower do, and **C#** and **Java** lowercase with the invariant culture.
- The consistency check follows **JavaScript**'s Number: a numeric string such as "1870.5" passes and prints as currency, while "R$ 350,00", which the MLX checkpoint writes for a medical receipt, an empty string, and null fail. A transcription converts strings the same way instead of demanding a number type.
- The currency comes from toLocaleString('pt-BR') with exactly two decimals, after R$ and a plain space. **Python**'s locale needs pt_BR installed on the machine, so it formats with a comma format and swaps the separators, **Go**, **Rust**, and **C++** group the digits by hand, and **C#**'s and **Java**'s currency formats put a non-breaking space after R$, so they format the number alone and prefix R$ and a space.
- The local companion runs as a process, python3 with its absolute path beside the program and no shell, its standard output trimmed is the answer, and a non-zero exit becomes the error with its standard error. Loading the checkpoint in process takes **MLX-LM**, a **Python** library, so every transcription keeps that contract, and the cases answer some calls with the real companion and others with a stand-in python3 that lets the transcription's own python3 through.
- The tuned endpoint's address comes from GOOGLE_VERTEX_BASE_URL, falling back to the regional endpoint, in whatever code makes the call. A transcription that imports the evaluation harness's transcription needs that harness to read the variable, since the original harness hardcodes the address.

## How close common targets get

The gateways are plain control flow around their model calls, so every target is a direct equivalent, with the clients of [llm-http-api](llm-http-api.md) and, for the assistant, a way to run gcloud and the local companion:

| Target                            | Fit               | Library                                                                                        |
| --------------------------------- | ----------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Python**                        | Direct equivalent | **ollama**, as the original, or urllib.request and subprocess for the assistant                |
| **JavaScript** and **TypeScript** | Direct equivalent | **ollama**, built with the host from OLLAMA_HOST, or fetch and child_process for the assistant |
| **Go**                            | Direct equivalent | net/http, encoding/json, bufio.Scanner for the stream, and os/exec                             |
| **Rust**                          | Direct equivalent | **reqwest** with blocking and JSON support, **serde_json**, and std::process::Command          |
| **C#**                            | Direct equivalent | HttpClient with ResponseHeadersRead, System.Text.Json, and System.Diagnostics.Process          |
| **Java**                          | Direct equivalent | The built-in HTTP client with BodyHandlers.ofLines, **Jackson**, and ProcessBuilder            |
| **C++**                           | Direct equivalent | **libcurl** with a write callback, **nlohmann/json**, and posix_spawnp with pipes              |

A tested **C#** transcription of the specific AI patterns lesson's gateway exists, built and run in the .NET 10 SDK container, and a tested **Python** transcription of the final project's assistant, which formats the currency by hand and runs the lesson's companion beside it as a process.

## Tolerance

The embeddings and the answers are replayed, so the gateway is deterministic and the cases compare its output exactly, the streamed drafts included, in either original's wording, with the self-check names free. The paraphrase hits the cache at 0.825 against a threshold of 0.75, and the closest miss scores 0.626, so the cache's decisions keep a wide margin. The answers come from gemma4:12b-mlx standing in for both models, so the drafts are its own, and every request is compared by structure: the model each intent picks, stream true, and both messages exactly. The tiering prototype's cases are replayed the same way, with gemma4:12b-mlx standing in for both tiers, and its volume case answers every concurrent copy of a request from one recording, checking the dispatch, the blocks, the tier decisions, and the closing summary by count instead of in order. The assistant's cloud answers are synthesized from **Vertex AI**'s generateContent format, since no tuned endpoint was reachable, and its local answers come from real runs of the module's MLX checkpoint, which repeat byte for byte with greedy generation, so its output is compared exactly, with the self-check names and the usage's command free.
